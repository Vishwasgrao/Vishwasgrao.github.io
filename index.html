My QA Journey

I didn’t start my career thinking I would redefine how quality is delivered. I started like most testers do—executing test cases, reporting defects, and following processes. But very early on, something didn’t sit right with me. Why were we always finding issues after they happened? Why was QA seen as a checkpoint instead of a driver of quality? That question changed everything. I stopped looking at testing as a task—and started treating it as a responsibility.

Over time, I moved beyond manual testing into automation, APIs, and performance. Not just to learn tools—but to solve a bigger problem: How do we build systems where defects struggle to exist in the first place? Working across global platforms in the UK, US, Germany, and Spain gave me more than exposure—it gave me perspective. Different systems, different users, different challenges—but one constant: Quality cannot be an afterthought.

The real turning point came when I stepped into UAT and production support. Handling live incidents—P0s, business-critical failures—there’s no hiding there. No theory. No assumptions. Just impact. That’s where I learned: Every missed edge case has a cost Every delay affects real users And every weak test strategy eventually shows up in production

So I changed how I approach QA. I stopped focusing on more testing And started focusing on smarter testing Prioritizing high-risk areas Strengthening automation where it actually matters Using data to guide decisions Preventing defects instead of reacting to them

Today, as a Senior QA Engineer, my role is not just to validate systems. It’s to build confidence in every release. To ensure that quality is not a phase—but a foundation. Because in the end, QA is not about finding bugs. It’s about making sure users never experience them.
